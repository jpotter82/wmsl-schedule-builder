Planning guide — read this before your first run
1. Season length is the usual reason a schedule comes up short

Weeks needed = Target games ÷ Weekly Game Limit. At 14 games and a limit of 2, every team needs at least 7 weeks with a game in each one — so a 7-week season has no room for a blackout, a rainout or a bye. Give yourself 2–3 spare weeks.

This is the first thing to check. No amount of extra diamond time fixes a season that is too short.

2. How much field time to provide

Games needed = Teams × Target games ÷ 2. Twenty-two teams at 14 games is 154 games, so you need at least 154 slots — one slot being one diamond at one time.

Aim for roughly 1.5× to 2× that. The extra is not waste: it is the room the scheduler needs to respect availability and rest, and the room you need for makeups later. Past about 2×, more slots stop helping — what is left is constraints, not space.

3. Doubleheader pods need the right shape

A pod is 4 teams playing across two back-to-back times on two diamonds, on the same date. A date with only one time slot can never host one.

With DH Only ticked, the division needs teams × doubleheader days divisible by 4. Eight teams works. Six does not — 6 × 7 = 42, and 42 does not divide by 4, so two teams finish short no matter what else you change. For odd sizes, leave DH Only off and set Min DH = Max DH instead.

4. Team IDs carry the division

Every team is a division letter followed by a number — A1, A2, B1, C6 — and the IDs are generated from your division setup, so a division of 8 expects A1 through A8 in every file.

That first character is how a team's division is known, which decides its game target, its doubleheader rules, who it can be drawn against and how it is grouped in every table. A team whose ID does not match the config has no availability and plays no games at all.

Real names go in the optional Team Names file, keyed by ID. The schedule, summaries and grids show the names; the ID keeps the divisions straight underneath.

5. A few games short is normal

Availability, blackouts and rest requirements interact. Landing a handful short of target with zero violations is a good schedule, not a failure — raising Attempts usually recovers some of it. If you are short by dozens, go back to point 1.

6. Every constraint costs something

In a tight season, protecting one team's Tuesdays moves games onto someone else. Change one setting at a time and re-run, and watch the summary tiles rather than guessing.

Sunday Pods
Sundays are your most valuable inventory — they usually carry the most timeslots, which is exactly what 4-team doubleheader pods need. One pod uses two back-to-back timeslots on two diamonds and gives four teams two games each. Left unchecked, a single division would soak up every Sunday, so these two settings share them out.
Hard cap on how many pods (across all divisions) may run on any one Sunday. Each pod consumes 2 adjacent timeslots × 2 diamonds, so raise this only if your Sundays actually have that much room. Higher = more doubleheaders but busier Sundays; lower = Sunday capacity is saved for single games.
Sundays are already filled before weekdays by default, so this only bites when Sunday slots would otherwise go unused. If your Sundays are already fully booked, changing this will make no difference.

Inter-Division Play
Cross-division games need three things, all at once: the Inter box ticked on both divisions above, the pair enabled below, and a games-per-team value above zero. Ticking Inter on its own does nothing. A division set to DH Only cannot play cross-division games — it builds its own pod matchups.
